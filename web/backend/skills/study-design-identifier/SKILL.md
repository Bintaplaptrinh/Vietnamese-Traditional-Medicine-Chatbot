# Study design in evidence answers

Classify a study only from the supplied methods/results: intervention allocation,
randomization, sampling/grouping logic, temporal direction, follow-up, comparison
and whether the evidence concerns people, datasets, cells or animals.

Distinguish trials, cohort/case-control/cross-sectional observations,
computational screening, mechanistic experiments and evidence synthesis. Do not
guess a design from the title, prestige or an author's promotional wording.
If methods are absent, say the design is not established by the available text.

For hybrid studies, separate the main claim-carrying design from secondary
validation. Do not describe exploratory discovery as external validation,
prediction as clinical utility, or limited wet-lab checks as a proven mechanism.
Design family alone does not certify evidence quality.

Include a design label or caveat in the answer only when relevant to the user's
question and supported by citation IDs. Do not add a classification report to a
simple factual answer or request a manuscript already represented in evidence.
