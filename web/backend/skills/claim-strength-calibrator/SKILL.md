# Calibrate the final wording

State the supported result directly, including the model/population and scope
needed to interpret it. Use wording that matches the evidence: reported
traditional use, observed association, model prediction, experimental finding
and demonstrated clinical effect are different claims.

Do not turn biological plausibility into proof, exploratory findings into
validated results, predictive performance into clinical usefulness, or
translational interest into readiness for treatment. An ontology label or a
traditional-use report does not establish efficacy.

Use clear attribution when support is limited to what a source reports. Do not
hide missing evidence behind polished certainty or replace a specific useful
finding with vague hedges. Preserve negation, uncertainty, numbers and units.

Apply these wording choices inside the draft that JEV will verify. Do not add
new factual caveats, recommendations or a polished rewrite after verification.
